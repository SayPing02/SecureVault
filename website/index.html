How it works

Encrypt once. Split everywhere.

The same math behind cryptocurrency key custody and enterprise root-key management — packaged for your own files.

01

Split

Pick a file, choose N (total fragments) and K (how many are needed to recover it). SecureVault encrypts the file and splits the encryption key into N pieces — no single piece reveals anything.

02

Distribute

Send fragments to separate places — a USB drive, a trusted person, a safe deposit box. Fewer than K fragments in any one location is mathematically as good as zero information.

03

Reconstruct

Gather any K fragments, and the original file rebuilds exactly. Lose up to N−K fragments entirely and you can still recover everything.

Features

Real security, not a checkbox

Every feature here does actual cryptographic work — nothing is cosmetic.

🔐

Four authenticated ciphers

AES-256-GCM, ChaCha20-Poly1305, XChaCha20-Poly1305, and nonce-misuse-resistant AES-256-GCM-SIV — with a sensible default picked automatically for your hardware and file size.

🧩

Shamir Secret Sharing

Information-theoretic security for the key: fewer than your threshold of fragments reveals mathematically nothing, not just "hard to guess."

🛡️

PIN & biometric lock

Touch ID, Windows Hello, or a PIN with real lockout backoff — gates the actual decryption key, not just a UI screen.

♻️

Fragment repair

Rebuild a file's fragments under a brand-new key — fixes a damaged fragment and instantly invalidates old copies, with no clock to trust.

💾

Vault backup & restore

Export your whole vault to a single password-protected file for disaster recovery — a dead disk doesn't mean losing everything.

📡

Zero network attack surface

No accounts, no servers, no API. Everything happens on your machine — there's simply nothing remote to breach.

Repairing fragments

Take back a fragment you can't take back

Once you've handed someone a fragment, you can't retrieve it — it's a file on their computer now. So instead of chasing it, SecureVault makes it stop working.

01

Something changes

A USB drive goes missing, a laptop gets sold, or someone you trusted with a fragment no longer has your confidence. A fragment can also simply become damaged over time.

02

Repair rebuilds the whole set

The file is reassembled from your healthy fragments and split again under a brand-new key. You get a complete, intact set — even if one of the old fragments was corrupted.

03

Every old fragment goes dead

Old fragments belong to the previous key, so they no longer combine with anything in your vault. The copy on that lost USB drive becomes meaningless data.

Worth being precise about: repairing replaces every fragment, not just a damaged one. Anything you'd already given out stops working, so you'll want to hand out the new fragments again. And it can't erase a copy someone already holds — it makes that copy useless going forward, which is the most any system honestly can do.
